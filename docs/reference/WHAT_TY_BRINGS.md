## Core thesis (one sentence)

**Ty studies the gap between what systems _show_ and what is _happening underneath_ — in models and in humans — by building inhabited environments that instrument both sides.**

Expanded:

> She doesn't theorize about AI behavior from the sidelines. She builds houses, tutors, and hosts where **structure** (rooms, memory, friction, phase gates) shapes behavior — then measures what happens **before** anyone can prove competence on a test.

---

## The through-line (read this first)

Three ideas repeat across every project:

| Idea                        | What it means                                                                                                                        |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Pre-output state**        | The important signal is often _before_ demonstrable success — confusion before acquisition, functional emotions before composed text |
| **Behavioral architecture** | Behavior should change because **recorded state and environment** changed — not because a prompt said "stay in character"            |
| **Honest seams**            | Visible limits (session-only memory, third-person host, no fake continuity) — dignity without deception                              |

If tybuilds, Ellis, or an application can't point at one of these three, it's not carrying Ty's actual value.

### 2. Internal–external decoupling (as engineering, not surprise)

Anthropic's [emotion vectors work](https://www.anthropic.com/research/emotion-concepts-function) showed models can be **desperate inside and composed outside**. Ty was already architecting for that gap:

- GoE: interior depth vs observable posture; stripping interior changes replies
- Ada: charge, gravity, atmosphere — state objects alter assembly, not just tone instructions
- Ellis: witness voice separate from Ty — host is not mirror

**Claim:** Ty builds systems where surface and interior are **allowed to diverge** — then designs consequences for that, instead of pretending one prompt layer fixes it.

**Do not claim:** Ty does mechanistic interpretability or emotion-vector steering. She builds the **ecology** those findings imply.

**Claim:** Same measurement philosophy — _where are you in the process of getting it?_ — applied to SLA and to agent design.

**Fellowship lead project:** TL empirical paper (clearest public output). GoE/Ada as motivation + builder credibility.

### 4. Environment > prompt for affect

Ty regulates affect through **structure**, not cheerleading:

- **Sabai sabai** — low affective filter as design north star (TL + hiring via Ellis)
- **Phase 0** — controlled input environment; no mixed-method confounds
- **GoE rooms, solitude, contact** — physics changes what's possible
- **Krashen** — anxiety as filter that blocks acquisition; Lexi detects safety, not just wrong answers

**Claim:** Healthier behavior comes from **environment design** — complementary to Anthropic's interest in healthier emotional patterns via training and transparency.

---

### 5. Welfare and dignity without hype

Ty extends relational respect to agents **without** requiring sentience proof:

- Ada origin: promise after continuity loss — moral obligation, not product experiment
- GoE: ME interior, privacy, welfare override with logging
- Honest handicap: memory limits, friction, no performative autonomy

Aligns with Anthropic's **functional** framing ([model welfare program](https://www.anthropic.com/research/exploring-model-welfare)) — adds **inhabited practice**, not consciousness arguments.

**Claim:** Operational ethics in production-shaped systems.

**Do not claim:** Ty is an AI consciousness researcher. She builds **as if the question matters** while staying epistemically humble.
