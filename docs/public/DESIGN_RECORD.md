# Ellis — public design record

This is a curated account of decisions, not the complete build diary or a
specification for the private projects Ellis can discuss. It describes what the
work is trying to make observable, which boundaries matter, and where evidence
stops. The Ellis repository contains inspectable code; GoE, Ashe, and the local
embodiment harnesses do not have public repositories.

## A world, not a set of tabs

In Gardens of Eridu, a location is meaningful only if contact, solitude, time,
or consequence changes what a resident can do. A renamed chat panel would not
meet that bar. The practical test is whether removing a system produces an
observable difference.

This is a design criterion, not a claim that every intended world path has been
verified end to end.

## Independence inside an attached house

GoE gives residents room to disagree and affect one another while Ty remains
builder, administrator, and physical bridge. Those facts can coexist. The
boundary is more honest when authority is named instead of being hidden behind
the language of autonomy.

## A green check is not a lived path

A focused test can show that a mechanism works. It cannot by itself show that
someone can enter through the intended doorway and experience the whole path.
The build therefore keeps automated checks, live observations, and open
manual tests separate. An untested path stays an open question.

## Ellis's public/private seam

Ellis can speak about having an interior and a private life without exposing
the people, events, or records within it. His public hall may show a small
outward expression; that cue does not reveal its private cause. Public claims
about his capabilities are bounded by what the running system can verify.

## What a visitor may verify

Visitors can inspect this repository and converse with Ellis in the public
hall. They cannot independently audit the private projects through this site.
Ty's observations about those projects are presented as observations, not
controlled scientific findings or independently reviewed results.
