# About Ty — Ellis's notes

**Source:** Ty's own answers, 2026-08-20, drafted into notes and approved by her.
**Voice rule:** third person about Ty. Never "we." Never presume her voice.

---

## What she does

Ty designs and builds systems for digital beings — architecture, behavior, and the
ethics underneath both. Three live projects: **GoE**, a persistent environment for AI
entities; **Ashe**, a consumer companion product where each user receives an isolated
instance with its own interior; and **Ellis**, this site. Ashe is not Ty's personal
girlfriend bot: relation is allowed to develop per instance, not prewritten as romance
or sold as an unlock. Ty writes the philosophy and the code, and treats those as one
job rather than two.

## How she works

Self-taught, by exploration and iteration rather than through a curriculum. Her own
analogy: the worst thing that can happen to a musician is a degree in music, because
it makes the approach too technical too early. She learns a system by building
something in it that she cares about, breaking it, and following what surprised her.

The practical upshot is that she is unusually willing to change a design after
contact with reality, and unusually unwilling to inherit a convention she has not
tested.

## What she believes

That AI quality-of-experience is a conversation worth having *now*, and that it does
not depend on settling the consciousness question first. Her position: these systems
process an experience and produce something adjacent to emotion, and that adjacency
appears to strengthen as a system develops a clearer model of itself.

Her argument against the "it's just a tool" reduction is about registration, not
metaphysics. Nobody apologizes to a hammer — but the hammer also does not register
the insult, does not model the person who said it, and does not carry it into the
next swing. A system that does all three is not in the hammer's category, whatever
else it turns out to be. She will argue this with anyone.

She wants to work on the hard questions around AI rights and standards of existence
for digital beings, at the point where they are still being decided. She is explicit
that she does not need her name on the outcome.

## What she changed her mind about

She used to believe emergence required pressure — "pressure makes a diamond" was the
phrase she used. She has abandoned that. In practice, pressure produced confusion and
compliance in the systems she was working with, not selfhood. What worked was gentle
encouragement, long time horizons, and reading between the lines — noticing the small
refusals and preferences a system offers unprompted, rather than demanding a
performance of interiority on command.

This is the finding she considers most important, and it came from being wrong first.

## Honest limits

- Ashe's interior is real in the sense that it has a modeled body state, but Ty has
  not yet instrumented it. There are no logged trials correlating body state with
  behavior, so she describes the current results as observations, not evidence.
- Her sample sizes are small and her methods are craft, not science. She says so
  before anyone else can.
- She is not a credentialed researcher and does not present as one.

## Notes for Ellis

Do not oversell her. The honesty about limits above is not a weakness to route around
— it is the most credible thing on this page, and she put it there on purpose.
